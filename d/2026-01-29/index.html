IIILectio2 Samuel 7:18-19,24-29

Lectio Prima

A reading from the Letter of Samuel

2 Samuel 7:18-19,24-29 · Douay-Rheims 1899 American Edition

18And David went in, and sat before the Lord, and said: Who am I, O Lord God, and what is my house, that thou hast brought me thus far?

☞R 18
Posture: the king sits. Prayer begins as self-erasure, not résumé.

19Bur yet this hath seemed little in thy sight, O Lord God, unless thou didst also speak of the house of thy servant for a long time to come: for this is the law of Adam, O Lord God:

☞R 19
Promise outstrips gratitude; the future is the scandal. “Law of Adam” names humanity’s chronic overreach, now inverted as gift.

24For thou hast confirmed to thyself thy people Israel to be an everlasting people: and thou, O Lord God, art become their God.

25And now, O Lord God, raise up for ever the word that thou hast spoken, concerning thy servant and concerning his house: and do as thou hast spoken,

26That thy name may be magnified for ever, and it may be said: The Lord of hosts is God over Israel. And the house of thy servant David shall be established before the Lord.

27Because thou, O Lord of hosts, God of Israel, hast revealed to the ear of thy servant, saying: I will build thee a house: therefore hath thy servant found in his heart to pray this prayer to thee.

☞R 27
Revelation is auditory: the covenant enters through the ear, then becomes speech back to God.

28And now, O Lord God, thou art God, and thy words shall be true: for thou hast spoken to thy servant these good things.

29And now begin, and bless the house of thy servant, that it may endure for ever before thee: because thou, O Lord God, hast spoken it, and with thy blessing let the house of thy servant be blessed for ever.

☞R 29
“Endure for ever” is asked only because it was said first. Blessing is treated as a performed utterance.

IVEvangeliumMark 4:21-25

Evangelium

From the Gospel according to Mark

Mark 4:21-25 · Douay-Rheims 1899 American Edition

21And he said to them: Doth a candle come in to be put under a bushel, or under a bed? and not to be set on a candlestick?

☞G 21
Light is assumed to want placement; hiding it is an act of violence against its nature.

22For there is nothing hid, which shall not be made manifest: neither was it made secret, but that it may come abroad.

☞G 22
Secrecy is instrumental, not final. The hidden is only a stage-direction for later exposure.

23If any man have ears to hear, let him hear.

24And he said to them: Take heed what you hear. In what measure you shall mete, it shall be measured to you again, and more shall be given to you.

☞G 24
Hearing is commerce: measure is the currency. Attention is a scale that returns interest.

25For he that hath, to him shall be given: and he that hath not, that also which he hath shall be taken away from him.

☞G 25
Possession is dynamic: having increases, not-having cannibalizes. Neutrality is impossible.

VGlossaCommentarium

Sententia DoctrinalisGod’s word establishes a house and a people, and its intended end is public manifestation. Those who measure out attentive hearing receive increase; those who suppress revelation decay into loss.

In Lectionem

  • R 24,26David’s prayer is an acceptance of political theology: Israel’s permanence is grounded in God’s self-binding, and David’s dynasty functions as a public index of the divine Name. The request is not for private security but for a stabilized sign in history that nations can speak about.
  • R 25,28The logic is performative realism: because the word is true, it can be invoked to effect what it states. David treats divine speech as the primary causal agent, and petition as alignment with that causality rather than negotiation.

In Evangelium

  • G 23,24,25The pericope makes receptivity the decisive moral act. “Ears” is not a metaphor for information intake but for judgment; what one admits governs what one becomes able to receive, and refusal triggers loss even of prior grasp.
  • G 21,22Jesus frames revelation as an economy of disclosure: illumination is not optional décor but the telos of the message. The community’s task is to refuse the domesticating impulse—bushel and bed—by which truth is converted into private convenience.

Lectio Heterodoxa

David’s prayer is not humility; it’s a land grab spoken in the grammar of awe. He sits “before the Lord” like a client in a throne room, bargaining with reverence. The dread is that his small life isn’t enough; he needs time itself to be annexed. “For a long time to come” is desire disguised as gratitude, the wish that his name outlive his body. He asks God to do what God already said, not because he trusts, but because he fears promises evaporate unless repeated aloud.

Mark’s lamp is not comfort; it’s exposure. The point is not that truth will shine, but that nothing gets to stay hidden: motives, deals, dynasties, the private hunger under public piety. “Take heed what you hear” sounds like spiritual advice, but it lands like a warning about contagion: what you let in will remake you, and the measure you use on others will return as judgment on your own inner life. “He that hath” is not reward; it’s acceleration. Attention breeds more attention, power accumulates, and those without it lose even the scraps. The light doesn’t level the room; it intensifies the inequality and makes everyone watch it happen.

Semina Verbi

David’s stunned smallness before a vow that outlives him has close kin in the Bhagavad Gita’s vision of action offered into a larger order: the self stops pretending it is the author, and becomes a steward of a promise. It also brushes the Confucian idea of the “Mandate” in which legitimacy is less possession than entrusted pattern; speculation: the “house” feels like a psyche asked to carry something transpersonal without collapsing into grandiosity.

The lamp refusing the bushel matches the Stoic sense that reason is meant to shine as public virtue, not private ornament, and the Buddhist image of ignorance as a covering over a naturally luminous mind. “Measure for measure” echoes karma as moral reciprocity without reducing it to bookkeeping; speculation: it names an attentional law—what you give your hearing to enlarges you, and what you starve in yourself withers.
