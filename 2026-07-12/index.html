    IIILectioIsaiah 55:10-11

    Lectio Prima

    A reading from the Book of Isaiah

    Isaiah 55:10-11 · Douay-Rheims 1899 American Edition

    10And as the rain and the snow come down from heaven, and return no more thither, but soak the earth, and water it, and make it to spring, and give seed to the sower, and bread to the eater:

    ☞R 10
    Rain does not negotiate; it descends, soaks, compels growth. Heaven’s gift is also earth’s invasion.

    11So shall my word be, which shall go forth from my mouth: it shall not return to me void, but it shall do whatsoever I please, and shall prosper in the things for which I sent it.

    ☞R 11
    The Word is a dispatched agent: sent, tasked, effective. “Void” is excluded by decree, not by reception.

    IVEvangeliumMatthew 13:1-23

    Evangelium

    From the Gospel according to Matthew

    Matthew 13:1-23 · Douay-Rheims 1899 American Edition

    1The same day Jesus going out of the house, sat by the sea side.

    2And great multitudes were gathered together unto him, so that he went up into a boat and sat: and all the multitude stood on the shore.

    3And he spoke to them many things in parables, saying: Behold the sower went forth to sow.

    ☞G 3
    The sower “went forth”: mission assumes loss. Proclamation is not optimized; it is broadcast.

    4And whilst he soweth some fell by the way side, and the birds of the air came and ate them up.

    ☞G 4
    Birds interpret first: theft precedes understanding. The surface is a feeding ground.

    5And other some fell upon stony ground, where they had not much earth: and they sprung up immediately, because they had no deepness of earth.

    ☞G 5
    Speed of sprouting is a symptom of shallowness. Heat reveals what haste concealed.

    6And when the sun was up they were scorched: and because they had not root, they withered away.

    ☞G 6
    Speed of sprouting is a symptom of shallowness. Heat reveals what haste concealed.

    7And others fell among thorns: and the thorns grew up and choked them.

    ☞G 7
    Thorns do not argue; they occupy. Competition kills without open hostility.

    8And others fell upon good ground: and they brought forth fruit, some an hundred fold, some sixty fold, and some thirty fold.

    ☞G 8
    One seed, unequal yields: fertility is not uniform. Abundance is graded, not binary.

    9He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.

    10And his disciples came and said to him: Why speakest thou to them in parables?

    11Who answered and said to them: Because to you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven: but to them it is not given.

    ☞G 11
    Knowledge is given, not earned; and it accumulates like capital. Lack is not neutral—it is confiscated.

    12For he that hath, to him shall be given, and he shall abound: but he that hath not, from him shall be taken away that also which he hath.

    ☞G 12
    Knowledge is given, not earned; and it accumulates like capital. Lack is not neutral—it is confiscated.

    13Therefore do I speak to them in parables: because seeing they see not, and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand.

    14And the prophecy of Isaias is fulfilled in them, who saith: By hearing you shall hear, and shall not understand: and seeing you shall see, and shall not perceive.

    15For the heart of this people is grown gross, and with their ears they have been dull of hearing, and their eyes they have shut: lest at any time they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and be converted, and I should heal them.

    16But blessed are your eyes, because they see, and your ears, because they hear.

    17For, amen, I say to you, many prophets and just men have desired to see the things that you see, and have not seen them: and to hear the things that you hear and have not heard them.

    18Hear you therefore the parable of the sower.

    19When any one heareth the word of the kingdom, and understandeth it not, there cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart: this is he that received the seed by the way side.

    20And he that received the seed upon stony ground, is he that heareth the word, and immediately receiveth it with joy.

    21Yet hath he not root in himself, but is only for a time: and when there ariseth tribulation and persecution because of the word, he is presently scandalized.

    ☞G 21
    Persecution is “because of the word”: the message generates its own weather. Scandal is the plant’s collapse under pressure.

    22And he that received the seed among thorns, is he that heareth the word, and the care of this world and the deceitfulness of riches choketh up the word, and he becometh fruitless.

    ☞G 22
    World-care and riches are paired: anxiety and allure, two hands on one throat. Fruitlessness is strangulation, not drought.

    23But he that received the seed upon good ground, is he that heareth the word, and understandeth, and beareth fruit, and yieldeth the one an hundredfold, and another sixty, and another thirty.

    VGlossaCommentarium

    Sententia DoctrinalisGod’s Word is efficacious act, not mere offer, and it both creates fruit and exposes resistance. The kingdom’s mysteries are granted, and that gift divides hearers into judgment and harvest.

    In Lectionem

    • R 10,11Isaiah frames revelation as a one-way efficacy: God’s speech is not information offered but action performed. The image collapses distance—heaven’s utterance becomes earth’s altered state—so “sending” names sovereignty over outcomes, not mere communication.

    In Evangelium

    • G 1,2,9,10,13,14Matthew stages a public/private divide: the same shoreline crowd hears, yet the discourse is deliberately indirect, and the disciples must ask for the key. Parables function as judgment as much as pedagogy—speech that discloses the kingdom while simultaneously exposing a prior incapacity to perceive, fulfilling Isaiah’s diagnosis of hardened perception.

    Lectio Heterodoxa

    Isaiah’s rain is not comfort but coercion: water that “returns no more” because it has already done its work, soaking, forcing, making the earth comply. The “word” is weather, not conversation. It doesn’t persuade; it saturates. It prospers because it is sent to prosper, and the ground’s consent is irrelevant.

    Matthew turns that same inevitability into a scene of selective violence. The sower is extravagant, almost careless, flinging seed where it cannot live, as if the waste is part of the design. The birds, the stones, the sun, the thorns are not accidents but the world’s honest organs, each with its own appetite. The parable is not a lesson for everyone; it is a filter that hardens the many into background noise while a few are made “blessed” by being chosen as soil.

    “Lest…they should…be converted, and I should heal them” is the cold line: healing is withheld to preserve a certain order of outcomes. Desire sharpens here. The crowd wants clarity, the disciples want privilege, and the speaker wants results. The seed that “springs up immediately” is the addict’s joy, the quick burn of meaning before the first heat wave. The “good ground” is not virtue; it is a temperament that can be colonized, deep enough to be occupied. Fruitfulness is not freedom. It is what happens when the word finally finds a body that cannot refuse it.

    Semina Verbi

    The rain-word that cannot come back empty resembles the Vedic notion of vāc as efficacious speech: utterance that does work in the world, not mere description. It also brushes the Tao Te Ching’s image of the Tao as water that nourishes without argument; speculation: both assume reality is most changed by what arrives softly and steadily.

    The sower’s mixed outcomes echo Buddhist karma as “seed” meeting conditions, and Yogācāra’s bīja stored in consciousness ripening when the field is ready; the birds and thorns read like the kleshas as predatory habits. Stoic prohairesis fits the soils as trained or untrained assent: impressions fall everywhere, but only a cultivated interior gives them root.

    Parable itself, as veiled speech that sorts listeners, parallels Zen’s koan and Sufi tales of Mulla Nasruddin: a teaching that withholds in order to expose the hearer. Speculation: the “mystery” is less hidden information than a diagnostic mirror—what you do with the story is the story’s real content.
